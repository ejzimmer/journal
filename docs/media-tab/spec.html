Media Tab Build Spec

Build the media tab as a bookcase. The working reference is The Media Bookcase — prototype.html, beside this file. It is interactive and its behaviour is normative: open it, and read it where this document is silent. This document says what the result must do, what has already been settled, and the things that will waste your afternoon if you meet them cold.

The outcome

Two bookcases, one for books and one for games, each a run of shelves that wrap.

  • Each series is a stretch of shelf with its name on a hand-written card clipped to the shelf edge. Books in a series share one trim height and carry matching bands at head and tail, so a set reads as a set.
  • Each spine shows the title and, on a second line, the author's full name. Spine height follows title length. The cover colour is the author, so an author's books look alike wherever they sit.
  • Books with no series collect in one unlabelled run at the end.
  • Status is a stamp at the foot of each spine that cycles when clicked. Unread stock sits pale on the shelf and takes its colour once finished.
  • Clicking the title opens the book in a dialog: title, status, author, series, and the series' band colour. A + beside each heading opens the same shape of dialog to add one.
  • Games get the same shelves, shorter and squatter, grouped and named by series.

Status cycle

Books: unread → 📖 reading → 🎧 listening → ✓ read → unread. Games: unplayed → 🎮 playing → ✓ played → unplayed. This is today's medium button with finished folded in; it reads and writes the medium and isDone already on the item. Unread shows the emoji greyscaled and faded; ✓ takes the series' band colour.

Order

Series runs are ordered by their author's name, so an author's series sit together. The singles run is ordered by author, then title.

Ground rules

Do these from the start and several whole categories of problem never arise.

  • Reuse the app's existing components — all of them. Combobox for the author and series fields, EditableText for renaming, FormModal and ModalDialog for the two dialogs, the existing button classes for buttons. Nothing here needs a new primitive. In particular: don't hand-roll a dialog (you will get focus and Escape wrong) and don't reach for a native <input list> + <datalist> — its popup is drawn by the browser, can't be styled, and follows the OS theme rather than the page.
  • Every colour in the tab is oklch(). Its lightness is perceptual, so holding L and C fixed and varying only the hue gives covers that genuinely look equally light. The same construction in HSL doesn't: a light yellow at the blue's lightness is a beige, and desaturating to compensate makes it worse. Skip hues near 100 entirely.
  • Two click targets per spine, neither nested. The title is a button that opens the dialog; the stamp is a button that changes status. The book as a whole is not a button.
  • Bands are decoration. pointer-events: none, no tooltip, no hit area — they sit over the title, and the card already names the series.
  • Spine height comes from title length, never randomised. An arbitrary height difference reads as meaningful and people will ask what it means.
  • Band colour is independent of cover colour. The cover is the author; a band drawn as a shade of it can't distinguish two series by the same author.

Shelf order is the name as typedAlphabetical on the author's name, so it files under whatever that begins with. No surname filing: a surname can't be derived from one free-text field, and a separate "file under" field isn't wanted.
No counts or progress statsNo "6 to go", no per-group progress pips, nothing that tallies. The shelf filling with colour is the only progress indicator.
Two new fonts are fineLiterata for spines, Dancing Script for the shelf cards. Add them alongside the four already loaded.
Series bands are always visiblePermanent, not revealed on hover — bands that appear on hover read as a glitch. The series is named on its shelf card, so the bands never need to carry the name themselves.
One dialog shape for add and editBoth are dialogs, not panels below the shelf. A button at the top with its form at the bottom of the page was tried and is worse.

Gotchas

These bite even when you've done everything above. The headline is the symptom, because that's how you'll meet it.

A two-line spine title reads backwards

Verifying by measuring x-positions will tell you it's correct, and it won't be. You read a spine by tipping your head right, which puts the rightmost column on top — so the first line must be on the right.

Use writing-mode: vertical-rl, not vertical-lr.

Long titles stop wrapping and grow past the shelf

Series members need min-height so a flex row with align-items: stretch can size them all to the tallest — but that leaves the height indefinite, so a percentage max-height on the label has nothing to resolve against and is ignored.

Cap the label with a definite length derived from the shelf, not max-height: 100%.

Every shelf board sits slightly above the books standing on it

The board is a repeating background gradient on the case, and backgrounds are measured from the padding box.

Space above the case must be margin, never padding.

The status stamp and the tail band overlap

Both want the foot of the spine.

Give the spine a deep bottom padding and place the stamp clear above the band, with the title clear above that.

Accessibility

  • Everything interactive is a real <button>. The spine's face carries the full name — "Guards! Guards!, Discworld series, read" — and the stamp announces its state and what a click will do next.
  • Bands are not focusable and take no pointer events. The series reaches keyboard users through the spine's accessible name and the shelf card.
  • Every title sits at 5.7:1 or better on its cover, unread included. Re-check if the cover lightness moves.
